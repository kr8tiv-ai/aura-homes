This pilot is an evidence record, not a storefront: stale claims stay labelled, regional gaps stay visible, and refuted leads remain in the record.

Read this before anything else

Aura facilitates. It does not sell homes, does not broker them, holds no custody of your funds at any point on this page, is not a party to any purchase you make, and carries no liability for it. It has no relationship — commercial, referral, or otherwise — with any company named here. They are listed because they are supported by a public acceptance claim or launch record. That is the entire basis for inclusion; it is not a vetted-seller designation.

Nothing on this page executes a transfer, quotes a rate, or takes a fee. Every leg of every route is something you do yourself, in your own wallet, with your own eyes on the address. Verify each claim at the source link on the card before you move any money — and treat a wallet address that arrives over email or chat as unverified until you have confirmed it by voice.

The guided route

From native USDC on X Layer to Crypto Emporium

This starts where you said the buyer starts: already holding native USDC on X Layer. It is a read-only mainnet route guide; Aura’s own checkout remains X Layer testnet-only. No swap, bridge or manufacturer payment is created here, and no API key or recipient address is trusted from page copy.

01

Prove the starting asset

The ticker is not enough

The intended source is native Circle USDC on X Layer mainnet (chain 196), contract 0xB6CEceAB302E2E4948951eE7843FC24E92933061. The legacy bridged token at 0x74b7F16337b8972027F6196A17a631aC6dE26d22 also reports “USDC”. A route is blocked until wallet chain, token contract, balance, destination asset, and recipient network are all exact.

Negative findings

What was checked and did not survive

A directory is only as trustworthy as the entries it threw away. These 13 leads were fetched and refuted — including the two that hurt most: Miradex, a genuine Romanian passive-house prefab maker whose only “crypto” matches were the word descriptor in Wix JavaScript, and Trademark Renovations in Calgary, whose 2018 headline is not a rail in 2026.

01

Miradex.ro (Romania — prefab timber-frame nZEB/Passive House kits): THE MOST IMPORTANT NEGATIVE. Cryptwerk lists it under construction companies accepting crypto, and it is a genuine eco-prefab maker (since 1992, 300+ projects, timber-frame house kits) — exactly the profile Aura wants. But its own site has zero crypto payment content. A regex on miradex.ro returned 16 apparent 'cripto' hits that are ALL inside the word 'des-cripto-r' in Wix JavaScript. Refuted.

02

High Performance Homes (Vancouver, WA): listed on the Cryptwerk construction directory; own site returned 0 visible crypto mentions. Also roofing/siding/windows, not homes. Refuted.

03

Energize Builders (Los Angeles): seed carried a B-press claim of '200+ crypto-funded projects'. Fetched energizebuilders.com — 0 visible crypto mentions on the live site. Refuted pending better evidence.

04

Trademark Renovations (Calgary, ALBERTA): the single most locally relevant lead in the whole sweep. CBC and the Globe and Mail reported in 2018 that it was the first Canadian general contractor to accept bitcoin and ether, and it does build custom homes. Its live site today mentions bitcoin, ethereum and cryptocurrency NOWHERE. Refuted — an eight-year-old headline, not a current rail.

05

cryptorealestate.com ('Crypto Real Estate'): Cryptwerk describes it as the largest global crypto real-estate marketplace with 1500+ listings. The live site is an unfinished GoDaddy template — 'Launching Soon' placeholders and stock filler copy ('Generate excitement', 'Say something interesting about your business here'). Refuted.

06

Naples Luxury Builders (Florida): seed listed it A-own-site for BTC. Could not confirm — naplesluxurybuilders.com returned an effectively empty body and the seed-adjacent domain napleluxurybuilders.com does not resolve. Unverifiable in this sweep; do not publish as a provider.

07

ADU Start (Canada): surfaces high in searches for Canadian crypto real estate, but it is an informational directory, not a builder, and accepts no crypto itself. Refuted.

08

Bookaglamping.com: DOES accept crypto (BTC, ETH, SOL, LTC, DOGE, XRP, BCH, USDC via BitPay) — but it sells glamping STAYS, not structures. Not a home provider; listed here so nobody re-checks it as one. Relevant only to the unique-stays rental use case as a demand-side comparable.

09

Cryptwerk 'Construction' directory as a source: 26 merchants listed, but every housing-relevant entry I spot-checked (Miradex, High Performance Homes) failed own-site verification, and the rest are trades — plumbing, roofing, fencing, painting, architecture. Treat the whole directory as C-tier lead generation only; never cite it as evidence of acceptance.

10

Baltic/Scandinavian prefab (Nordland House, Modhouse, Dolena, SPARS AP, VIA-S, Bitus): searched specifically because the jurisdictions are crypto-forward. No crypto payment claim found for any prefab manufacturer in Estonia, Latvia or Lithuania. Nothing to verify.

11

Container-home makers (Backcountry Containers, ROXBOX, Amish Built Cabins, Sweet-Haus, Glitzcamp): searched the container and tiny-home angle. The only container/crypto overlap is companies selling containers FOR bitcoin mining, not residential container homes sold FOR bitcoin. Refuted.

12

A-frame and kit-cabin sellers (Avrame, Den, 7 Day A-Frames, DC Structures, Backcountry Hut Co., Lushna): searched the A-frame/kit-cabin and glamping-pod angle explicitly. No crypto acceptance found for any of them.

13

NOT RE-VERIFIED IN THIS SWEEP — do not publish these as providers without a live fetch: Gafisa (Brazil, B-press via Foxbit), DAMAC (Dubai, B-press), and Wallaya Villas (Phuket, seed already had a 403). I ran out of sweep on these three; they carry over as open, not as confirmed.
