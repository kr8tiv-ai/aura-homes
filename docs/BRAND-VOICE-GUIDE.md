# The Aura Homes voice

*A brand voice guide. Written August 13, 2026, from primary research (sources
at the end), a full inventory of the live site's copy, and the founder's
strategic-messaging brief — validated where it held, corrected where it
didn't. This document changes no code and no copy; every rewrite in §8 is a
suggestion awaiting a yes.*

---

## 0. The voice in one paragraph

Aura talks like a good builder friend who happens to read the code — the
building code and the Solidity. Relaxed, because we know what we know.
Precise, because a home is a six-figure decision and vague words cost real
money. Funny where fun is free, never where risk lives. And honest to the
point of being a little disarming: we publish the number before you ask,
including the ones that don't flatter us. Nothing cheesy — no exclamation
points doing the work adjectives couldn't, no "reimagined," no "unlock your
dream." The dream is a house. We help you plan the house.

## 1. The problems we solve — and the proof they're real

Every claim below was researched against named sources (list in §10). This
is the load-bearing spine: if a sentence on the site doesn't trace to one of
these problems, it's probably decoration.

**1. Planning is where builds bleed.** Cost overruns are the industry norm,
not the exception — roughly 9 in 10 construction projects run over, with
average overruns around 28%, and about a third of overruns trace to
estimating errors made before anyone breaks ground. Owner-builders fare no
better: a 500-person survey put 62.5% over budget. **VALIDATED.**
→ *This is why the honest cost model, the reconciled-to-the-dollar budget,
and "an estimate, not a quote" exist.*

**2. Land surprises kill small builds.** US regulation adds a
builder-association-estimated $131,734 to a new home (26.4% of the average
price), with ~$47K incurred during land development alone; failed perc
tests, minimum dwelling sizes, and septic-setback stacking are the
documented rural traps. **PARTIALLY VALIDATED** (mechanism overwhelming,
single killer stat elusive — quote the mechanism, attribute the number).
→ *This is why the land-fit pilot exists, and why its rejection with a
cited rule is the most valuable screen in the product.*

**3. The demand is real.** ADU permits are up 1,100% from 2015 to 2025 and
now make up 7% of US residential permits (government-derived data — use
this, not tiny-home market-size dollars, which vary wildly by research
house). A-frame and prefab interest is documented and growing. **VALIDATED.**

**4. Contracting has a trust deficit.** The FTC logged 81,925 home
improvement scam reports in 2024; the category perennially tops consumer
complaint rankings. The failure mode isn't missing star ratings — scammers
have ratings — it's unverifiable claims. **VALIDATED.**
→ *This is why we do evidence with dates and expiries, and why "Aura does
not label providers as vetted" is a feature, not a hedge.*

**5. Crypto audiences reward receipts and punish overclaiming.** Tokenized
RWA grew ~5x in three years to ~$31B on public chains; post-FTX, published
proofs became the trust standard. The counter-case is written in court
records: Celsius led with "safe as a bank" and ended with a $4.7B judgment
and a 12-year sentence. **VALIDATED.**
→ *"Planned, not live" isn't just ethics. It's the empirically survivable
strategy.*

**6. Boring honesty compounds.** Patagonia told people not to buy the
jacket and grew ~30%. 37signals wrote "underdo the competition" and has
been profitable for 25 years. Berkshire opens its letters with its
mistakes. **VALIDATED** (causality caveats noted in sources).
→ *Our honesty pass is not a compliance chore. It's the brand.*

## 2. Two audiences, one product truth

The research on brands that serve a mainstream and a technical audience at
once (Discord, Notion, Figma, and Robinhood as the cautionary tale) agrees
on one mechanic: **one shared artifact, role-specific lenses — never two
stories.** Figma's model fits us exactly: the home plan is the file; the
everyday planner and the crypto-native look at the same truth through
different panels, and neither view lies to the other.

### The eco-side reader (the pragmatist)
Wants: a real home, real costs, no surprises on the land, and to keep the
equity a coordination margin would eat. Skeptical of green hyperbole and of
crypto in equal measure. Reads "USDC" above the fold as a reason to leave.
**Rule: crypto stays silent on this path until the reader chooses it —
already a product invariant (beat 6, one $HOMES link at the very end).
Defend it.**

### The crypto-side reader (the participant)
Fluent in the culture; allergic to being marketed at. Wants mechanism,
receipts, and a trajectory worth being early to. For this reader, the
tightened labels — *experimental, planned, zero records, proves execution
not facts* — are not disclaimers. They're the alpha. Nobody else in the RWA
lane talks like this, and this reader knows exactly why that matters.
**Rule: full depth, full culture-fluency, zero yield-speak.**

### What "talk about the trust structure" means now
The founder's ask — the crypto side should show the benefits and talk about
the trust structure for the token, what it does — collides gently with the
founder's own newest edits (the phrase "decentralized property trust" is
now test-banned on /roadmap, and the legal vehicle is stated as undecided).
The resolution is to talk about it **functionally, in the conditional, with
the label attached**:

> *The planned HOMES structure, in plain words: Aura would earn small,
> disclosed fees. Most of them (60%) would flow to a fund that buys and
> operates real eco stays. The people who hold and stake HOMES would share
> in the net profit of those stays, paid in USDC on X Layer, with every
> payout carrying a receipt. If it never reaches viability, the earmarked
> balance has a published path back to holders. None of this is live. The
> legal vehicle and participant rights are still being designed — and we
> won't pretend otherwise, because the projects that pretended are court
> cases now.*

That paragraph is the template: **what it would do** (fees → fund → homes →
stays → receipts), **who it would serve**, **the wind-down**, **the label**,
and **why the label is the point** — no securities language, no promised
returns, no structure-name the site has retired.

## 3. Voice principles

Synthesized from the benchmarks (Tesla's spec-forward declaratives, Oxide
and 37signals' published reasoning, IKEA's zero-condescension warmth,
Coinbase's define-it-inline discipline, Base's locked vocabulary, Airbnb's
name-the-reader's-situation) — filtered through what already works on our
own best lines.

**P1 — Let the number carry the sentence.** "Fits a 1,076 sq ft minimum"
beats "rigorous compliance checking." When a stat does the work, adjectives
are freeloaders. *(Tesla, minus the timeline bravado.)*

**P2 — Publish the unflattering number first.** Winter AWG output: zero
litres. The registry: five blockers, so we held. Estimate, not a quote.
Saying it before being asked is the whole trust engine. *(Patagonia,
without the activism costume.)*

**P3 — Show your working.** Behind every honest surface is a public doc —
cost basis, plan provenance, the mainnet brief. Link to it. Teaching is our
marketing budget. *(Oxide/37signals.)*

**P4 — Warm imperative, reason attached.** "Check the perc test first —
everything else depends on it." Instructions for adults, never baby-talk,
never lecture. *(IKEA.)*

**P5 — Define crypto terms in the sentence, not a glossary.** "USDC (a
dollar-pegged token)" the first time, every surface, both journeys. A
reader should never need a tab open to finish our sentence. *(Coinbase.)*

**P6 — Name the reader's situation before the feature.** "You found a
parcel you love. Here's what to check before you fall further." *(Airbnb's
clarity — not its belonging-poetry; our community is still future tense.)*

**P7 — Fun rides on precision, never on risk.** "Gas is cheap. Trust is
not." is our register: a smile built out of true things. Jokes never appear
within a sentence of money, land, law, or the token. *(The Robinhood
lesson: simplify language, never risk disclosure.)*

**P8 — The label is part of the sentence.** *Planned. Experimental.
Demonstration. Estimate.* These aren't footnotes; they're adjectives that
travel with their nouns, everywhere, both journeys, no exceptions.

### Cheese guardrails (the "never" list)
- No "reimagined," "revolutionize," "unlock," "elevate," "dream home
  awaits," "the future of X is here."
- No Apple-fragment cosplay ("Eco. Homes. Rethought.") — we borrowed their
  concision, not their cadence.
- No belonging-poetry we can't back with humans ("join the family").
- No superlatives we'd have to defend in court ("safest," "guaranteed,"
  "the most advanced ever").
- No crypto hype dialect on ANY surface (moon, ape, degen, ser) — the
  crypto reader respects fluency, not pandering. Culture-fluent means we
  know the room; it doesn't mean we perform it.
- No exclamation points except in genuine celebration of the USER's moment
  (their plan, their milestone) — and even then, one.

## 4. The vocabulary contract

Base publishes an editorial style guide that locks a handful of words; it's
the cheapest brand-consistency tool that exists. Ours, derived from the
founder's newest edits (several already test-pinned) plus the consistency
audit in §7:

### Locked terms (one name per thing — pick these, retire the rest)
| The thing | Its ONE name | Retire (found in the audit) |
|---|---|---|
| The land tool | **the land-fit pilot** (hyphenated) | "land fit pilot", "land discovery", "Find land" as a feature name |
| The design tool | **the builder** (verb: *design*) | "questionnaire", mixed Build/Design/Shape labels |
| The saved thing | **your project record** | workspace/document/book of evidence/local project (8 names today) |
| The /homes surface | **the HOMES ledger** | dashboard, zero-state ledger, public property ledger |
| The HOMES idea | **the planned HOMES structure**; plain-words: *"an Airbnb its guests and hosts own"* | "decentralized property trust" (banned on /roadmap; still live on 4 other surfaces — unify) |
| The chain lab | **the X Layer testnet lab** (*experimental*) | "proof lab with public receipts" (banned) |
| Status taxonomy | **Today / Next / Future** (everywhere, incl. /roadmap, which currently says "Now") | Now/Later variants |
| The start CTA | **Start your project** | 6 variants today |
| Non-live data | **demonstration** | sample/demo/fixture in user-facing copy |

### Standing labels (mandatory adjectives)
`planned` · `experimental` · `demonstration` · `estimate, not a quote` ·
`proves transaction execution, not lifecycle facts` · `not live` ·
`legal vehicle undecided`

### Banned (with the guard that enforces each, where one exists)
"vetted" as a designation · "decentralized property trust" on /roadmap
(recommend: site-wide) · "native USDC" · "buy a home with USDC" · blanket
"Open source (MIT)" (software is MIT; plan studies keep their licences) ·
"$85M"-era financing claims · "real code checks" · yield/APY/return
language for HOMES, anywhere, ever.

### Ticker discipline
"$HOMES" appears exactly once on the eco journey (the end link — by
founder design). On the crypto journey the ticker is at home. Never in
education pages' body text; there it's "HOMES" the concept.

## 5. The founder's strategy brief — validated, corrected, adopted

The "Strategic Messaging Refinement" brief was researched against sources
and the repo's own truth ledger. Verdicts:

**ADOPTED (it's right, and now it's doctrine):**
- **Invisible Web3 / crypto as optional plumbing.** Confirmed by every
  successful two-audience case studied. The site's architecture already
  does this; the copy should finish the job. "Cash works. Crypto is
  optional plumbing." is approved-voice and worth surfacing.
- **Lead with lifestyle + economics on the eco path; mechanism on demand.**
- **"Ownership" as the bridge word** — physical title for one reader,
  participation for the other. Elegant and honest.
- **Today/Tomorrow separation for HOMES** — matches our Today/Next/Future
  invariant.
- **The coordination-margin story** — the strongest economic hook we have,
  *when quoted at the real numbers* (below).

**CORRECTED (right idea, wrong facts):**
- The brief's owner-built triplet "$199,100 / $301,280 / $443,900" does not
  match the repo's anchored cost model: **$195,250 / $295,680 / $434,700**
  (ex-land, data/alberta/cost-model.json, reconciled by test). Only the
  anchored numbers may appear in copy.
- "Up to 40% coordination margins" — unsourced. Say what we can defend:
  *"a conventional builder delivers this reference home at $450–650K
  ex-land; owner-coordinated, the same home computes to $195–435K on our
  published model"* and let the reader subtract.
- "Traditional builders stack up to 40%… hire **vetted** experts" — uses a
  banned word; the corrected line is "hire licensed trades against an
  evidence file."

**REJECTED (conflicts with the truth ledger — do not use):**
- "Aura utilizes advanced smart contracts to hold funds safely in escrow,
  releasing payments when milestones are met" — describes a LIVE escrow
  service. Nothing is live; the lab is experimental with zero records; the
  founder retired escrow as a product. The honest version: *"we've proven
  the mechanism on testnet — and we'll tell you exactly what that does and
  doesn't mean."*
- "Every contractor is evaluated through an immutable evidence file… against
  a tamper-proof ledger" — evidence lives in the local project record, not
  on-chain; "Aura does not label providers as vetted" is site law.
- Silent wallet provisioning / Stripe fiat-conversion checkout — future
  architecture; must not leak into present-tense copy.
- Its hero suggestion "Architected by AI. Owned by You." — capitalized
  You-cosplay plus an AI-first frame the founder's verbatim hero already
  beats. The current hero stays.

## 6. Journey rules

### Eco journey (gate → beats → end)
1. Crypto-silent until beat 6; one $HOMES note at the end. (Invariant —
   defend in review.)
2. Every beat: situation → what Aura does → what Aura does *not* do, in
   under 60 words. The "does not" clause is the signature.
3. CTAs are verbs a non-technical adult would say aloud: *Design my home ·
   Find land · See likely costs · Start your project.*
4. **One leak to fix (suggestion only):** the gate proof line — "OKX Build
   X · AI Season 2026 · X Layer testnet · HOMES trust and owner launchpad
   planned" — is the first sentence BOTH audiences read, and it's pure
   crypto jargon before the visitor has chosen a door. See §8.1.

### Crypto journey
1. Open with what's real (the lab, the hashes, the open repo), then the
   planned structure per the §2 template, then the ask (follow the build).
2. Every claim wears Today/Next/Future. Zeros render honestly and say why
   that's the feature.
3. Depth is respect: fee splits, staking design, wind-down mechanics all
   belong here — in the conditional, sourced to the concept doc.
4. Culture-fluency means precision the room respects (block numbers, OKLink
   receipts, "we held mainnet and published why") — not slang.

### Education pages
Define-inline rule (P5) is absolute. "You never need crypto to use Aura"
stays the first sentence of /how-crypto-works.

## 7. Site journey vs. this guide — the gap analysis

From the verbatim inventory (August 13). The good news first: the bones are
excellent. The founder-verbatim hero, the two-door gate, the honesty
invariants, and lines like *"Aura keeps the record straight, not the
hammer"* and *"No proof, no claim"* ARE the voice. The gaps are consistency
and density, not direction.

**Gap 1 — Name sprawl.** The land tool has 6 names, HOMES has 8, the
project record has 8, the start CTA has 6, and /build answers to five
different verbs. §4's contract resolves each. Cost of fixing: label edits
only.

**Gap 2 — Three status vocabularies for one "product invariant."**
Today/Next/Future (education, story) vs Now/Next/Future (/roadmap) vs
Now/Next/Later (story rollout line). Unify on Today/Next/Future.

**Gap 3 — The jargon airlock leaks.** "X Layer testnet," "HOMES trust,"
and "owner launchpad" hit every visitor at the gate; "RWA" is never
expanded anywhere; "SIP" is unexpanded on the landing scene; "WCB" never
expands. The eco journey's crypto-silence is real *after* the gate but not
*at* it.

**Gap 4 — Density cliffs on /homes.** The plain-words band (recently
added) is exactly right — and then 44-word five-clause sentences, "AMM spot
price," "Uniswap V3 position NFT," and an imperative addressed to token
engineers ("Do not embed a permanent max-wallet restriction…") follow in
the same scroll. The ledger needs a consistent two-layer pattern: plain
sentence first, precise mechanics under it (or behind a disclosure).

**Gap 5 — Drift the codebase already knows how to kill.** The boundary
sentence exists in four near-copies ("Designs are…" vs "Drawings are…") —
the exact drift class lib/stamp.ts was built to eliminate for drawings.
Same cure: one exported constant. Likewise "25-plan library" is hardcoded
on /roadmap while PlanCatalog refuses, on principle, to type a number.

**Gap 6 — Stale doors.** /faq still links "the overview" (/overview, a
redirect stub); /build offers "the questionnaire" (/design → a loop back to
/build). Two link edits.

**Gap 7 — One banned-phrase near-miss.** The story end sheet still says
"Open source, end to end — MIT on GitHub" (Story.tsx:695); the release-truth
test guards other files but not this one. Should match the adopted "MIT
software · Plan studies keep their listed licences."

**Gap 8 — "Payments" labels a page with no payment surface.** The eco end
sheet's link to /buy says "Payments." Rename to what the page is:
"Explore homes."

## 9. The pre-publish checklist

Before any sentence ships, anywhere (site, X, docs):

1. **Would it survive the truth ledger?** (Nothing unbuilt in present
   tense; labels attached.)
2. **Does the number trace?** (Only anchored or attributed figures; no
   orphan stats.)
3. **Did we say what it does NOT do?** (The signature clause.)
4. **Would the other audience misread it?** (Eco reader: no unglossed
   crypto. Crypto reader: no yield-speak.)
5. **Is the fun anywhere near the money?** (If yes, cut the fun.)
6. **One name per thing?** (Check §4.)
7. **Could a regulator read it aloud in a hearing without us wincing?**
   (The Celsius test.)
