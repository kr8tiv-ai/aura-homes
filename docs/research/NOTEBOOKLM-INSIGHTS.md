## Contradictions with our current build

1. **"SIPs are the best wall system" — overstated.** ICF walls hit RSI 4.22 (R-24) vs our 6½" SIP's RSI 3.43 (R-19.5); wrapped wood-frame hits R-20.3–21.4. SIP joints/splines are code-recognized thermal bridges (manufacturer framing factors required in the effective-RSI math), SIPs have low drying potential if moisture gets past the barrier, and on-site assembly is weather-exposed with every joint SCO-inspected. SIP remains defensible for our no-basement, high-design use case — but the docs should stop claiming thermal superiority and start engineering the joints and the floor.
2. **"Off-grid all-electric with wood-stove heat" fights Alberta physics and economics.** Alberta's grid is 80%+ fossil (0.85 t CO₂e/MWh — ~5x on-site gas per MWh); electricity costs 2.5–3x gas per unit of energy; going off-grid forfeits the 35¢ Solar Club export rate that makes solar pencil; and manually-fired wood stoves don't satisfy Hot2000 mechanical-efficiency inputs for automated permitting (a compliant thermostatic system must be modeled as primary). The ERA-proven affordable path is grid-tied "Net-Zero Carbon Ready" (+$30K) — awkwardly, with a gas furnace. Resolution to consider: keep off-grid as the flagship *option* with honest cost deltas, make grid-optional the default recommendation, and model the wood stove as secondary heat.
3. **"No concrete anywhere" survives — with riders we haven't budgeted.** Screw piles are recognized permanent foundations for Part 9 and A277, but they are a Part 4 element: P.Eng-stamped plans + field verification letter are mandatory (not in our cost model), piles must reach 8–15+ ft (and 2–3 ft below frost line because wind-washing strips the insulating snow under an elevated home), the RSI 5.02 floor requirement bites hard, and we give up the full-basement option that drives RTM resale value in prairie markets.
4. **"Crypto escrow as a selling point" — inverted.** The founder's own Strategic Blueprint says mainstream buyers are "completely indifferent" to smart contracts and drop off when wallets appear; AEC practitioner studies score "trust in automated judgments" lowest; mortgage-financed buyers need price stability. The escrow is real differentiation for judges and crypto-native early adopters — but consumer marketing must sell milestone protection and holdback compliance, never the chain. (Our card-first requirement already points this way; the notebook hands us the concrete rail: Privy/Web3Auth MPC + sponsored gas + flat CAD display.)
5. **"The AI is the architect" — legally impossible as stated.** AI has no standing under the Safety Codes Act, cannot be licensed, and cannot execute Schedules A/B; every envelope schedule and Part 4 element needs a human professional's cryptographic seal (APEGA/AAA joint bulletin, May 2024, governs exactly who). Our "review-ready design package" framing is correct — keep tightening the language everywhere "AI architect" appears, and productize the human seal (P.Tech/P.Eng + Notarius) as a platform feature instead of a caveat.
6. **"Stunning architectural glass" collides with net-zero-ready math.** Best triple-pane glass ≈ R-5 vs R-35–40 NZR walls (7–8x worse per m²); high FDWR inflates heating loads and summer overheating (mechanical cooling), and prairie wind/cladding pressures make big panes structurally expensive. The performance path CAN approve high-glazing designs (that's already our plan) — but each glass wall costs envelope budget elsewhere; the design engine should price FDWR in real time and steer glazing to the view elevation only.
7. **Site-built SIP vs A277 factory modular — the notebook leans modular; our docs lean site-built.** Factory A277 units skip on-site envelope inspection (foundation + utilities only), build dry, and are what Alberta's existing manufacturers (Modern Modular, ROC, Built Prefab) actually produce — but post-factory modification voids certification, shipping caps width ≈2.6 m (killing some architectural forms), and permits must precede fabrication regardless. This is a genuine strategic fork to decide deliberately: SIP kit + site assembly (design freedom, our current bet) vs A277 partnership (speed, inspection relief, financing familiarity) — or offer both tracks in the catalog.
8. **Minor but real: "AWG standard on every home" gets zero support here.** Nothing in these 74 sources addresses atmospheric water generation — the notebook neither rescues nor re-condemns it. Our FEASIBILITY demotion stands as the honest position.
